## Week 1 — The pullback: a trade that works

**Adam H. Grimes · 2014 · added** — [Read the post](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/pullback-trade-works/).

Useful alongside lesson 02 because it describes trends as impulses interrupted by corrections. It offers a vocabulary comparison outside SMC and encourages attention to the overall movement. Treat its edge claims as the author’s position, not validation of your strategy.

**After reading:** Label five impulses and pullbacks without using “institutional,” “manipulation” or order-block terminology. Write the price event that would invalidate each pullback hypothesis.

<a id="nesting"></a>
## Week 2 — The nested pullback

**Adam H. Grimes · 2014 · added** — [Read the post](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/nested-pullback/).

A visual explanation of a smaller pause inside the move following a larger pullback. Compare its nesting idea with lessons 10 and 14 while preserving the workbook’s pivot-confirmation rules.

**After reading:** Annotate the larger swing and smaller pause on one chart. Compare a lower timeframe, and explain which structural level belongs to which view.

<a id="fvgs"></a>
## Week 3 — Understanding Fair Value Gaps

**TTrades · workbook** — [Read the article](https://ttrades.com/understanding-fair-value-gaps/).

A visual companion to lesson 06 covering the three-candle pattern, midpoint, inversions and distinctions from other gap labels. Its invalidation conventions need to be written down before you begin a sample.

**After reading:** Mark five candidate FVGs manually. Record the three candles, zone boundaries, confirmation time and the precise rule you will use for failure. Include a failed example.

<a id="pois"></a>
## Week 4 — The Only Points of Interest That Actually Matter For Trading

**TTrades · workbook** — [Read the article](https://ttrades.com/the-only-points-of-interest-that-actually-matter-for-trading/).

Useful as an example of an ordered zone-selection process. The title expresses this educator’s framework; it does not establish that other zones never matter. Its CISD material can be revisited after lesson 19.

**After reading:** Choose at most two candidate zones on a chart, explain the ranking and reject one with a written reason. Keep zone selection separate from entry confirmation.

<a id="bias"></a>
## Week 5 — Easy Daily Bias: A Mechanical Trading Framework

**TTrades · workbook** — [Read the article](https://ttrades.com/easy-daily-bias-a-mechanical-trading-framework/).

Provides one framework using candle closes, wicks, equilibrium and lower-timeframe confirmation. Some linked concepts go beyond this week; use the article to understand the structure of a hypothesis and revisit unfamiliar CISD details in week 6.

**After reading:** Write bullish, bearish and neutral conditions in a [session plan](../templates/SESSION_PLAN.md). Make sure unfinished daily-candle information cannot leak into your pre-session decision.

<a id="fibonacci"></a>
## Week 6 — Reader question: what’s wrong with Fibonacci?

**Adam H. Grimes · 2016 · added** — [Read the post](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/whats-wrong-fibonacci/).

A useful counterpoint to lesson 22. It questions flexible measurements and assumed significance of ratios. Read it as a reason to specify and compare filters, while retaining the workbook’s Fibonacci lesson.

**After reading:** Define identical anchors for two development tests: your baseline and the same baseline with an OTE filter. Record excluded opportunities as well as taken ones. Reserve fresh data for evaluation.

<a id="model"></a>
## Week 7 — A Simple 3-Step Trading Model

**TTrades with AM Trades · workbook** — [Read the article](https://ttrades.com/a-simple-3-step-trading-model-full-breakdown/).

An example of connecting daily context, a time framework and lower-timeframe execution. Its seven-hour profile is part of that public model, not an added requirement for this curriculum or a reconstruction of the private instructor’s personal strategy.

**After reading:** Separate the model into context, location, trigger, invalidation and exit. Note any missing definition rather than filling it with an unrelated rule.

<a id="backtesting"></a>
## Week 8 — Successful Backtesting of Algorithmic Trading Strategies, Part I

**QuantStart · added** — [Read the tutorial](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/Successful-Backtesting-of-Algorithmic-Trading-Strategies-Part-I/).

The discussion of optimization and look-ahead bias applies to manual replay as well as code. Programming is optional for this roadmap. Access was intermittent during research; use the workbook’s [backtesting article](https://ttrades.com/how-to-backtest-a-clear-step-by-step-approach/) and the local [testing protocol](../practice/TESTING.md) if it is unavailable.

**After reading:** Audit three possible leaks: swings confirmed by future bars, finished higher-timeframe candles used too early, and a test period selected after seeing winning setups.

## Throughout — Constructing an Effective Trading Journal

**Brett Steenbarger / TraderFeed, with Danny Shcharinsky · 2015 · added** — [Read the post](https://traderfeed.blogspot.com/2015/03/best-practices-in-trading-constructing.html).

Useful from week 1 because it frames a journal as a way to observe and improve your decisions. Pair reflection with the workbook’s objective evidence and trade records.

**After reading:** Add three lines to the [weekly review](../templates/WEEKLY_REVIEW.md): one observation about your behavior, one goal and one concrete next-session action.

## Optional — Relative Strength & Weakness + SMT Divergence

**TTrades · 2026 · added** — [Read the article](https://ttrades.com/relative-strength-weakness-smt-divergence-full-guide/).

Offers a public interpretation of divergence and relative structure across related instruments. This helps with the catalog’s SMT topic, but does not supply the course’s specific “SMT Fill” rules or validate a correlation assumption for every market.

**After reading:** Use matching timestamps and clearly defined comparison swings. Save a divergence, a non-divergence and an ambiguous case. Keep this as observation until the filter is fully specified.
