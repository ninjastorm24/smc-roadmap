# Candle Range Theory: separate the anchor, sweep and confirmation

A public CRT walkthrough, with the missing TBS component kept explicit.

**Lesson 21 · Week 7 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/21-crt-tbs.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-7)

An anchor candle gives you a high and low that can be marked before the next interaction. Candle Range Theory uses that range as the starting reference for a sweep-and-return scenario. The useful question is which later event qualifies, and what happens if price keeps moving beyond the range instead.

The workbook pairs CRT with TBS. It provides a path for studying public CRT ideas but does not provide the instructor’s TBS definition. A standalone CRT research model and the exact combined course model must therefore remain distinguishable.

## Choose the anchor before the outcome

Specify the timeframe, session boundary and completed candle you will use. If the anchor is selected because its range later contains a dramatic reversal, you have already used outcome information.

Record the completed high and low and the earliest moment the range is available. Do not use an unfinished higher-timeframe candle’s final boundaries while replaying earlier lower-timeframe action.

Public CRT explainers, including the workbook’s [CRT Trading guide](https://www.crttrading.com/), describe particular variants. Their existence does not establish one universal timing convention or a validated win rate.

## A hypothetical sweep-and-return sequence

Suppose the selected completed anchor spans 100–110. Price later trades to 98 and a qualifying candle closes at 102. Under an illustrative close-back-inside rule, the low-side sweep and return condition has occurred.

That event is separate from any lower-timeframe structure confirmation. If your proposed model also requires a close above a confirmed minor high at 104, it remains pending until that condition occurs.

The opposing anchor boundary at 110 may be a candidate objective. Whether your entry leaves enough room relative to stop distance, costs and the model’s target rule must be checked separately.

## Define the alternate outcomes

A candle that closes below 100 has not met this example’s return condition. A later reclaim can be studied only if it remains inside the permitted window. Price might also sweep both boundaries, never revisit an entry zone or invalidate the model before a fill.

Choose the rule for those cases before reviewing the chart. Do not select whichever candle close makes the eventual winner qualify.

## What TBS still needs

Record the original instructor’s required context, reference level, trigger, invalidation and expiry when available. Until then, label any tested public variant by its actual rules. Do not call an ordinary MSS filter “TBS” simply because the combined name appears in the course.

## Practice the sequence in stages

Collect twenty anchor interactions and timestamp anchor completion, excursion, return, optional confirmation and expiry. Save breakout continuations alongside successful returns. Your first conclusion should explain which stages were observable and repeatable; performance claims require the later controlled testing process.
