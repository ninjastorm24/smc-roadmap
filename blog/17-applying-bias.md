# From daily bias to a session plan someone else can follow

Connect the context chart, selected zone and entry condition on one page.

**Lesson 17 · Week 5 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/17-applying-bias.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-5)

A good session plan reduces the number of decisions you must invent while price is moving. It connects a broad hypothesis to a specific observation and states what happens if that observation never arrives.

The workbook’s top-down workflow is deliberately practical: start with the daily context, identify hourly structure and POIs, then use a lower timeframe for execution conditions. Each transition should add detail without rewriting the earlier evidence.

## Build the plan from the top down

On the context chart, identify confirmed extremes, the relevant dealing range and possible external objective. State bullish, bearish or neutral, and write the event that would change that classification.

On the setup chart, identify the structure and no more than the permitted number of POIs. Record boundaries and why an alternative was rejected. Only then move to the execution chart to identify the exact trigger.

The [TTrades top-down explanation](https://ttrades.com/mastering-top-down-analysis-aligning-multiple-timeframes-for-expansion-trading/) is a useful companion. Your written rules still determine what each timeframe can authorize.

## A hypothetical plan in five sentences

“On the context chart, the upward hypothesis remains valid until the selected close-based condition below 100 occurs. My preselected objective is 120. The qualifying setup zone is 107–109, confirmed before this session. On the execution chart, I require a completed close above the selected confirmed minor high after a permitted zone interaction. If that sequence does not complete inside the observation window, I record no entry.”

This example is a proposed planning structure, not a complete strategy. An actual test still needs entry-price assumptions, sizing, stop, target, expiry and costs. The important point is that each sentence has a separate job.

## Plan the absence of a trade

Common exclusions include conflicting structural rules, a missing stop reference, an expired window, an event restriction or insufficient room to the chosen objective under your model. Define those conditions before the session.

“No trade” should identify a failed or missing prerequisite. It should not become a vague judgment applied differently after wins and losses. If you decline an eligible setup because you were distracted, record a missed opportunity rather than rewriting eligibility.

## Preserve the before image

Save the plan and screenshot at the information cutoff. During the session, append changes with timestamps. Afterward, save a second screenshot and compare three outcomes: the bias hypothesis, the existence of an eligible entry and your adherence.

A useful review might say: “The directional objective was reached, but the confirmation occurred after expiry, so the model correctly generated no entry.” Another might identify a rule violation during a profitable simulation. Both are clearer than judging the session only by P&L.

Complete ten plans using the same chart pairing. Ask whether a second reading can identify the trigger, invalidation and no-entry conditions without asking what you meant.
