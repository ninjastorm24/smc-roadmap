# FVG, inverse FVG and order blocks: three ideas, three rule sets

Separate a visible gap, its later role change and a candle-based zone.

**Lesson 06 · Week 3 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/06-fvg-ifvg-order-block-imbalance.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-3)

A fast move can leave several attractive markings on the same chart. You may see a three-candle gap, the last opposing candle and a later revisit. Calling all of them “imbalance” hides the differences you need for testing.

The workbook puts these concepts together so you can compare their formation and failure rules. A visual zone identifies something to observe. A complete entry model requires additional decisions.

## Define the three-candle gap

For a bullish FVG, candle three’s low is above candle one’s high. For a bearish FVG, candle three’s high is below candle one’s low. The interval between those prices defines the candidate gap. It becomes knowable only when the third candle meets your completed-candle rule.

In a hypothetical bullish example, candle one has a high of 100 and candle three has a low of 103. The zone runs from 100 to 103, with midpoint 101.5. Price traveled during the middle candle, so this is not evidence that the entire interval had no transactions.

The [TTrades FVG article](https://ttrades.com/understanding-fair-value-gaps/) distinguishes this pattern from other gap labels. Keep that visual definition separate from claims about what price must do next.

## A fill and an inversion are different observations

A revisit can touch part of a gap, reach its midpoint, pass through it or never occur within your observation period. Define the outcome categories before collecting examples.

An inverse FVG adds a role-change hypothesis after the original zone fails. For a proposed bullish-to-bearish inversion rule, you might require a close through the original gap and a later qualifying retest from below. The exact close threshold, expiry and retest criteria must be specified. A mere touch does not establish all of those events.

Record the original formation and later failure times separately. A zone cannot be used as an inverse setup before the inversion conditions exist.

## An order block uses a different reference

The workbook introduces the last opposing candle before directional expansion as an OB candidate. That selects a candle-based zone rather than the non-overlap between candle one and candle three.

A hypothetical OB may span 98–101 while a nearby FVG spans 100–103. Their intersection is 100–101, but neither their overlap nor the shared expansion proves independent confirmations. Record each qualification separately.

## Practice formation before reaction

On twenty candidates, mark the formation candles, boundaries and first confirmation time. Choose a fixed observation window and record full failure, partial revisit and no revisit. Do not silently remove gaps that never offer an entry.

Your first useful result is consistent identification. Only after you can reproduce that result should you ask whether a specific entry rule using the zones adds value after costs.
