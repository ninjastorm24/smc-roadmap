# Quarterly theory: make the time hypothesis falsifiable

Specify quarter boundaries and count the sessions that do not follow the expected sequence.

**Lesson 23 · Week 6 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/23-quarterly-theory.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-6)

Dividing a period into four parts is straightforward. Predicting how price behaves in those parts is a separate claim. Quarterly theory becomes a useful research topic when you keep those two tasks distinct.

The workbook presents a four-part time framework, often associated with Daye, with proposed accumulation, manipulation, distribution and continuation/reversal labels. Treat those labels as hypotheses to define before the chart unfolds.

## Fix the clock before assigning phases

Specify the cycle you are studying, its start, end, timezone and quarter boundaries. A calendar month and a fixed-duration session need different boundary rules; do not silently treat them as interchangeable.

If the source uses a “true open,” record exactly which opening event supplies it. Different public variants can disagree on anchors. Keep each variant in its own definition record instead of combining whichever boundaries fit a particular day.

[Quantum Algo’s quarterly-theory guide](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/ict-quarterly-theory/) is one public explanation linked by the workbook. Its proposed schedule does not establish a validated predictive edge.

## A hypothetical time-segmentation exercise

Suppose you choose a four-hour observation window from 08:00 to 12:00 in a stated market timezone. Dividing that window into four equal hours gives you Q1 through Q4. This is an invented timing exercise, not a claim about the course’s exact quarterly schedule.

Before replay, define what a range, excursion and expansion would look like under your rules. Then log when each event actually occurs. If the main expansion happens in Q1 rather than the anticipated Q3, preserve the contradiction.

Moving the cycle start after seeing that expansion would create a new model. It cannot repair the original prediction retroactively.

## Compare against an appropriate baseline

A timing filter can change opportunity count and the types of market behavior sampled. To understand that change, compare the same defined setup with and without the timing condition on development data.

Record no-signal quarters and excluded opportunities. If the time filter appears helpful, check a later reserved period using unchanged rules. Use enough observation time for the model’s frequency; a handful of clean days is not a stable estimate.

Scheduled events and daylight-saving changes also need dated treatment. Do not attribute every volatility difference to the quarter label when another documented timing factor is present.

## Make room for failure

The framework can be investigated only if some outcomes count against it. Write those outcomes down: wrong phase order, absent expansion, a signal outside the allowed quarter or no improvement over the baseline after costs.

Your deliverable is a timeline with original expectations and observed events. A clear record of nonconforming days is valuable because it prevents the theory from becoming an explanation that can accommodate every possible chart.
