# Pools, voids and premium/discount: organize the range before the zones

Distinguish clusters of levels, fast price movement and relative range position.

**Lesson 13 · Week 3 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/13-liquidity-pools-ranges.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-3)

A liquidity pool, a liquidity void and a discount zone describe different things. One concerns clustered references, another describes a stretch of price behavior, and the third locates price within a chosen range. They can appear together without becoming the same concept.

Studying them separately makes it easier to explain which observation supports a scenario and which condition remains missing.

## Pools are reference clusters

Equal or near-equal highs and lows can form a visible cluster. Record how close the levels must be to count as equal and when each becomes confirmed. A tolerance expressed in ticks is reproducible; “roughly the same” needs refinement.

The pool label is a hypothesis about possible order concentration. It does not measure the actual resting orders. For chart work, record the cluster’s prices and what happens when price approaches them.

## Voids describe an area differently from FVGs

The workbook distinguishes a broader rapid run or low-activity interpretation from the specific three-candle FVG rule. Do not infer measured trading volume solely from the visual speed of candles.

If you mark a void, state its start, end and formation convention. If you mark an FVG, identify its three candles and non-overlap boundaries. A broad run can contain several FVGs or none under the selected timeframe and rule.

Supply/demand zones introduce another selection method: areas preceding directional movement. Compare their boundaries and prerequisites with OB candidates rather than treating every preceding candle as both automatically.

## Premium and discount require anchors

For a hypothetical range from 100 to 120, equilibrium is 110. Prices above 110 are in the range’s premium half; prices below it are in its discount half. This is relative position, not a valuation model or an automatic trading instruction.

Change the range to 108–128 and equilibrium becomes 118. Price 114 is premium in the first range and discount in the second. Both calculations are correct; the unresolved question is which anchor rule your analysis uses.

The workbook’s [premium/discount resource](https://ttrades.com/understanding-premium-and-discount-in-trading/) provides a public explanation. Lock the range-selection convention before testing any condition based on its midpoint.

## Combine observations into a falsifiable scenario

An educational scenario might say: “Within the confirmed 100–120 range, I will observe a defined bullish POI below 110 after a qualifying interaction with a marked low. A close below the stated invalidation cancels the scenario.”

That sentence still needs exact POI, confirmation and exit rules before it becomes a trade model. It does, however, show how each concept contributes a different condition.

Practice by drawing ten ranges and recording when their anchors became confirmed. Add pools and voids afterward. If the midpoint changes, log the rule that permitted the update instead of redrawing the range simply to improve the explanation of a finished move.
