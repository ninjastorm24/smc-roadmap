# Advanced order blocks: improve the definition before adding filters

Boundaries, retests and a rubric you can apply before a reaction.

**Lesson 07 · Week 4 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/07-advanced-order-block.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-4)

An order block can look precise because a rectangle has exact edges. The selection process behind that rectangle may still be subjective. Which opposing candle counts? Is its body or full wick used? What expansion makes it qualify? What cancels it?

Advanced OB work begins with these questions. More filters are useful only when they make a decision clearer or improve evidence under a controlled comparison.

## Freeze the basic candidate rule

The workbook’s starting point is an opposing candle before directional expansion, interpreted within structural context. Specify whether you select a single candle or a sequence and how the qualifying move is recognized.

Then select a boundary convention: full candle, body, or another explicitly sourced refinement. An educator’s midpoint rule is one possible convention, not a universal requirement. [TTrades’ OB explanation](https://ttrades.com/order-blocks-simplified/) is a public source to compare against your written definition.

In a hypothetical bullish example, the candidate’s low is 98, open 101, close 99 and high 102. A full-range zone is 98–102; a body zone is 99–101. These are different test models if they alter entry, stop or qualification.

## Separate qualification from what happened later

A candidate should qualify because its formation conditions were satisfied. It should not become “valid” only after it produces a successful reaction.

Record subsequent behavior independently: no revisit, first revisit, repeated revisit, expiration or invalidation. An unrevisited zone is a real observation even though it creates no fill. A failed zone remains part of the formation sample.

Specify what a new visit means. Multiple candles inside one continuous interaction may not be separate retests under your chosen rule. Without a visit definition, “freshness” can become another flexible label.

## Build a short rubric

For a learning exercise, choose a few mandatory questions: did the structural prerequisite occur, are boundaries reproducible, and is invalidation clear? Keep optional context such as premium/discount or an earlier sweep in separate columns.

This avoids hiding a weak candidate behind a total score. It also lets you later compare a baseline with one optional filter. If you add every favorable-looking condition at once, you cannot tell which change affected the sample.

For each rejected candidate, save the specific failed condition. For each accepted candidate, record it before the reaction. Revisit the same charts later to check that you choose the same candle and edges.

## What counts as progress

A useful OB worksheet should let you point to a candidate, its confirmation time, boundaries, permitted revisit and failure rule without seeing the future. That is a concrete improvement in research quality even if the first tested entry model loses money.
