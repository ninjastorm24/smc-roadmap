# Rejection blocks, breakers and Unicorns: follow the formation sequence

Tell a wick-based area apart from a failed zone and a breaker–FVG overlap.

**Lesson 08 · Week 4 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/08-rejection-breaker-unicorn.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-4)

Three rectangles can occupy roughly the same price area while representing different ideas. One is selected from a rejection wick, another from a failed zone and another from overlapping structures. The geometry alone does not explain which interpretation you are using.

The workbook groups rejection blocks, breaker blocks and the Unicorn model together. Study the event sequence for each before comparing outcomes.

## A rejection area begins with a wick convention

A rejection-block interpretation emphasizes a wick-centered area after rejection from a price extreme. Naming and boundaries vary across educators. Record which candle supplies the wick, whether its full length is used and when the area qualifies.

A long wick can be described directly: price traded beyond an earlier reference and closed elsewhere in the candle’s range. That observation does not, by itself, reveal the participants or establish a later reversal.

For a hypothetical candle with high 110 and body top 106, your proposed upper-wick area might be 106–110. If that is your rule, a later test cannot quietly shorten the zone to exclude a losing touch.

## A breaker needs a prior sequence

The workbook introduces a breaker as a previously qualified OB that fails and is later considered from the other side, within a structural sequence. Identify the original candidate, its violation and the required revisit. The [TTrades breaker article](https://ttrades.com/breaker-blocks-simplified/) illustrates one public interpretation.

Suppose a bearish candidate spans 104–106. Price later closes above the required boundary and confirms your bullish structural condition. A return toward 104–106 can now be studied as a bullish breaker candidate. The earlier bearish zone and the later bullish hypothesis have different eligibility times.

If the confirming break never occurred, the role change has not qualified under this rule. If price never revisits, there is no assumed entry.

## A Unicorn adds an overlap condition

The workbook describes a Unicorn as a breaker/FVG overlap. With a breaker at 104–106 and qualifying bullish FVG at 105–108, the intersection is 105–106.

That narrower interval is easy to draw. It still needs an entry rule, expiry, invalidation and fill assumption. For example, a limit order at the overlap and a market order after a confirming close can produce different trades even though both reference the same area.

Also notice that the breaker and FVG may both depend on the same expansion. Their agreement is not automatically two independent pieces of evidence.

## Compare what each condition changes

Use separate columns for rejection-zone qualification, breaker qualification and FVG overlap. Include sequences with no overlap, no revisit and failed entries. Choose the sample dates before selecting attractive examples.

The exercise should reveal what the overlap actually contributes: clearer location, fewer opportunities, different fills, or no measurable improvement. The name is useful only when the underlying conditions remain reproducible.
