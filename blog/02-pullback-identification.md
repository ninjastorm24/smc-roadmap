# Reading pullbacks without predicting every reversal

A practical way to compare an impulse, its correction and the level that challenges it.

**Lesson 02 · Week 1 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/02-pullback-identification.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-1)

Price rises, pauses and drops a little. Is that a pullback, or has the trend reversed? The chart does not answer by giving the movement a name. You need a reference move and a rule for deciding what would contradict your interpretation.

A pullback is a correction relative to an earlier directional move. Its meaning depends on the structure you are studying. A decline can be a reversal of a small upward swing while remaining a correction inside a larger advance.

## Define the move before its correction

Begin by marking the directional leg and the confirmed structural level it depends on. Then describe what the correction has done: how far it traveled, how much it overlapped earlier candles and which known levels it crossed.

Do not make candle color your definition. One red candle can occur inside a continuing advance, and a sequence of green candles can occur during a broader decline. The relationship between swings is more informative than the number of candles of each color.

The workbook asks you to compare shallow, deep and overlapping corrections. Those descriptions are useful observations, but they do not become predictive categories until you define their boundaries and test them.

## A hypothetical sequence

Suppose your chosen structure rises from 100 to 110, corrects to 106 and then reaches 114. The 106 low is now important under your current swing rule. Price subsequently falls to 109.

| Observation | What it supports | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|
| Price is below 114 | A correction is underway | Whether it will become a reversal |
| The confirmed 106 low remains intact | The larger upward interpretation remains possible | Whether a new advance will occur |
| A completed candle closes below 106 | The interpretation fails under a close-based rule | Whether the next regime will trend downward or range |

These are invented levels illustrating a proposed rule. If you use a different structural scale or wick-based invalidation, your classification can differ. Record that difference before seeing the outcome.

## Decide when the correction has ended

Possible research rules include a close above a confirmed minor high, a new higher low followed by a break, or a measurable expansion candle. Each has a confirmation delay. An earlier signal can also fail; a later signal can miss part of the move.

Select one rule for an exercise and record the first candle that satisfies it. Do not use the later rally to move that signal backward. [Adam Grimes’s pullback article](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/pullback-trade-works/) offers a complementary description of impulse and retracement; its performance claims do not substitute for your own sample.

## Practice the uncertain cases

Collect rising, falling and ranging examples. Before advancing the chart, write: “I interpret this as a correction of ___ until ___ occurs.” Save cases where the condition fails and cases where the expected continuation never develops.

Your goal is a repeatable interpretation with a clear expiration condition. Directional certainty is not required to complete the exercise.
