# Advanced entries, part 2: audit the trigger instead of stacking labels

Engulfing, iFVG, BPR and timing combinations need observable rules and documented gaps.

**Lesson 25 · Week 8 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/25-advanced-entries-part-2.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-8)

A setup description can become more impressive while becoming less executable. Adding an engulfing candle, a session condition and several acronyms may leave you with more ways to explain a winner and no clearer instruction before the entry.

The second entry lab asks you to compare confirmation and timing families while controlling complexity. The most useful question is: what exact event changes the decision from waiting to eligible?

## Engulfing requires a boundary convention

Decide whether engulfing refers to bodies or full candle ranges, whether strict inequality is required and whether the current candle must close. A body-engulfing pattern and a full-range outside bar select different candles.

For hypothetical bodies of 100–102 and 99–103, the second contains the first. That does not establish a specific trading edge or reproduce the course’s advanced variants. Context, direction, order, stop and expiry still need definition.

## Inversion and rejection require a sequence

An iFVG entry first needs the original gap, its defined failure and a qualifying role-change interaction. A rejection entry needs a measurable reaction or confirmation condition. The fact that both labels appear near one zone does not make their triggers identical.

The workbook’s [iFVG article](https://ttrades.com/how-to-trade-inversion-fair-value-gaps/) is a public companion. Preserve the exact close and boundary conventions you use rather than choosing them after the result.

## A public BPR example

One public convention defines a balanced price range (BPR) as the overlap between opposing FVGs. [The Right Trader’s BPR explanation](https://therighttrader.com/learn/smart-money-concepts/balanced-price-range) describes this construction. It is a related public concept, not a definition of the course’s OC/BPR entry.

For a hypothetical bullish gap at 100–103 and later bearish gap at 102–105, the intersection is 102–103. Both gaps must first qualify under their own completed-candle rules. Until the later gap exists, this overlap cannot be used as a BPR reference.

The overlap still needs an allowed direction, trigger, expiry and invalidation before it becomes a trade model. If the gaps do not overlap, this particular construction produces no BPR. Do not widen their boundaries to create one.

OC, CiC and the other source-specific abbreviations remain unresolved here.

## Check whether the conditions add distinct information

If a displacement candle both breaks structure and creates an FVG, those labels depend on a shared event. Adding them together does not automatically multiply confidence.

A useful comparison keeps the baseline fixed and changes one condition at a time on development data. Record excluded opportunities and transaction costs. Do not keep searching for filters against the same evaluation sample and continue calling it untouched.

Use ten blind examples to compare your two clearest model cards. Record disagreement before viewing outcomes. If the disagreement concerns what the trigger even means, repair that definition before interpreting which candidate won more often.
