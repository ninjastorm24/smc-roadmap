# Price across timeframes: keep the information clock aligned

Why nested structure matters, and how unfinished higher-timeframe candles can mislead a replay.

**Lesson 14 · Week 2 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/14-fractal-price.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-2)

An hourly decline and a five-minute rally can happen at the same time. The smaller rally may be a correction inside the larger move. Timeframe analysis helps you preserve both descriptions while deciding which one governs a particular task.

The workbook calls this the fractal nature of price. For learning, focus on nesting and consistent information timing rather than assuming that a pattern performs identically at every scale.

## Give each timeframe a role

Choose a context chart for the broad hypothesis, a setup chart for location and an execution chart for precise conditions. The workbook uses daily, hourly and five- or fifteen-minute combinations as examples. Your actual pairing should fit your observation schedule and instrument.

Write down what each chart is allowed to change. A lower-timeframe event might cancel an execution condition while leaving the context hypothesis intact. A confirmed violation of the chosen context level might require rebuilding the broader plan.

## A hypothetical nested move

Suppose an hourly upswing runs from 100 to 120, then corrects toward 112. Inside that correction, the five-minute chart may form lower highs and lower lows. A small bullish shift near 112 could end the local decline without proving that the hourly advance will resume.

Keep those statements separate. The local event becomes one candidate execution condition, while the hourly hypothesis retains its own invalidation. If the charts conflict under your rules, waiting is a valid output.

## Synchronize what was available

A finished hourly candle contains lower-timeframe activity that happened throughout the hour. At 10:15, the final 10:00–11:00 hourly high, low and close are not yet available.

A replay that shows the finished hourly candle beside the 10:15 five-minute chart can accidentally reveal later information. Use synchronized replay when available, or explicitly track the evolving higher-timeframe candle. If your rule requires the hourly close, wait for that close.

This timing issue also affects pivots. An hourly swing may need later hourly candles for confirmation. Its exact historical price can be visible on a lower timeframe before its hourly structural role is confirmed.

## Use a three-column explanation

For each example, record the context state, setup state and execution state at one common timestamp. Then add the level or event that would change each state. Avoid switching timeframe after an unfavorable outcome merely because another chart presents a better story.

Save ten cases, including a lower-timeframe reversal that fails, one that remains a higher-timeframe pullback and one that develops into a broader change. Your aim is a coherent decision process across scales, not a requirement that every timeframe point in the same direction at all times.
