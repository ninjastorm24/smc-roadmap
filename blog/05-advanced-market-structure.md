# Market structure across two scales: BOS, CHoCH and protected levels

Build an external map while keeping internal changes in their proper context.

**Lesson 05 · Week 2 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/05-advanced-market-structure.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-2)

A five-minute chart can look bearish while an hourly chart still looks bullish. Both descriptions can be internally consistent. The problem begins when a small structural break is allowed to rewrite a larger thesis without a rule explaining why.

Advanced structure study starts by keeping the scale of each claim visible. A label should identify its reference swing, timeframe, confirmation convention and implication for the existing map.

## External and internal describe relationships

Your external structure is the swing framework selected for the current analysis. Internal structure consists of movements inside that framework. A particular pivot can be external on one map and internal on another; its role comes from the map, not a permanent property of the price.

Before adding BOS or CHoCH labels, mark the external high and low and the last confirmed change to that range. Then add internal swings using the same confirmation discipline you learned earlier.

## Define the break before naming it

The workbook uses BOS for a continuation break and CHoCH or MSS for a countertrend structural event, while acknowledging source variation. State whether your rule needs a wick penetration or a completed close. If displacement is required, define how you measure it rather than relying on visual excitement.

For a hypothetical upward framework, a close above the selected external high might be a continuation BOS. A close below the selected protected low might challenge that framework. Crossing a minor internal low is a different event unless your rules explicitly promote it.

The [TTrades structure guide](https://ttrades.com/advanced-market-structure-simplified-from-short-term-to-long-term-trends/) provides a public explanation of swing hierarchy. Your own map still needs fixed operational criteria.

## Give protected levels a job

A protected level is useful when it states what would invalidate a structural interpretation. Write both the level and the required event. “Below 106” is incomplete if you have not decided whether that means any trade, an intrabar wick or a closing price.

Consider an hourly upswing whose selected protected low is 100. Inside it, five-minute price moves from 110 to 108 and breaks a minor low at 109. The internal map has changed; the hourly invalidation condition has not necessarily occurred.

You can therefore write two statements: “The hourly upward hypothesis remains valid under rule A; the five-minute execution condition is unavailable under rule B.” That gives you a coherent reason to wait.

## Review the map in chronological order

Advance one candle at a time and preserve earlier labels. Record when a new swing becomes confirmed, when a break qualifies and when your framework changes. A finished chart should show the history of those decisions, including failed breaks.

If a second marking pass produces a different external range, inspect the selection rule. The next improvement may be clearer swing selection rather than another confirmation indicator.
