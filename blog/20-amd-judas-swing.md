# AMD and the Judas Swing: keep the unfinished day unfinished

Study range, excursion and expansion without forcing every session into the story.

**Lesson 20 · Week 6 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/20-amd-judas-swing.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-6)

After a session ends, it can be easy to divide the chart into a quiet range, a false move and the main directional expansion. The harder question is what could have been recognized while the sequence was still developing.

The workbook studies AMD and the Judas Swing as scenarios with observable events and alternate outcomes. The names should not cause the eventual daily high or low to appear knowable earlier than it was.

## Translate AMD into observations

AMD stands for accumulation, manipulation and distribution in this teaching framework. For research, describe the underlying prices: a range, an excursion beyond a reference and a later directional move.

Define the initial range’s time window and boundaries. Specify what excursion qualifies and what later event identifies expansion. If those definitions depend on whichever part of the finished chart looks quietest, the scenario cannot be applied consistently.

[TTrades’ Power of Three article](https://ttrades.com/ict-power-of-three-amd-accumulation-manipulation-distribution-explained/) is a public explanation of the narrative. The phase names do not independently identify the market participants or their intent.

## A Judas Swing needs more than a failed breakout

The workbook treats a Judas Swing as an early-session move through a visible reference that subsequently reverses. The selected session, opening reference, recovery condition and confirmation window need to be defined.

For a hypothetical exercise, the preselected range is 100–105. Price trades to 99, then closes back above 100. Your close-back condition may now qualify. You still need any separately required structure shift or entry trigger.

If price instead closes below 100 and expands toward 95, record the continuation alternative. If it reclaims 100 but later stalls without a permitted entry, record that outcome too.

## Do not assign the final phase in advance

At the first excursion, you do not yet know whether the day will deliver a reversal, continuation or extended range. A note can say “possible reversal sequence; confirmation pending.” That preserves uncertainty without losing the reference level.

Likewise, calling the opening range “accumulation” does not prove buying or selling by a particular group. A descriptive range rule is enough to study the pattern’s behavior.

## Count non-fitting days

Select a continuous set of sessions and define an observation window. For each, record initial range formation, excursion, reclaim, confirmation and any eligible entry as separate events.

Some sessions will form no clear range. Some will break both sides. Others will continue through the first broken boundary. Retain them rather than adjusting the phase labels until the narrative fits.

Your review should tell you which event was usable in real time and how often the full sequence was absent. That is stronger evidence than a collection of completed charts whose phases look obvious afterward.
