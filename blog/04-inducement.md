# Inducement: turn a persuasive story into a rule you can inspect

How to study IDM without relabeling every disappointing move as a trap.

**Lesson 04 · Week 1 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/04-inducement.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-1)

Inducement is an easy label to attach after a reversal. A minor level breaks, price changes direction, and the entire move gets described as a trap. That explanation may sound coherent while telling you very little about what could have been identified beforehand.

The workbook treats IDM as a source-dependent interpretation. Your task is to define the proposed level, its relationship to larger structure and the price event that would support or reject the hypothesis.

## A minor swing is not automatically IDM

Start with the structural facts. Which external range are you using? Which confirmed minor swing sits inside it? Which larger objective is relevant? These can be marked without asserting anything about unseen participants.

One public interpretation describes a smaller swing in front of a larger potential liquidity reference. [The Right Trader’s IDM guide](https://therighttrader.com/learn/smart-money-concepts/inducement) teaches such a relationship. Other educators use different requirements, so record the source rather than merging definitions.

A higher low can remain a valid structural higher low without meeting your IDM criteria. The additional label must contribute an observable distinction. Otherwise it simply renames information you already had.

## A hypothetical classification rule

Suppose an external upswing spans 100 to 120. A confirmed internal low forms at 112, and price is currently above it. For this exercise, you propose calling 112 an IDM candidate only if a specified larger upside reference remains untested and your fixed internal-swing rule selects that low.

You then separately record whether price:

1. Remains above 112 and reaches the larger objective.
2. Trades below 112, reclaims it under your chosen close rule and later advances.
3. Trades below 112 and continues downward.
4. Produces an unresolved sequence within your observation window.

The proposed rule is an educational classification, not the instructor’s private definition. Notice that it includes a continuation without a sweep and a break without recovery. Both prevent the theory from winning by definition.

## Keep the label before the outcome

Save the candidate screenshot before revealing later candles. Write its selection reason and the window over which you will observe it. Once the window ends, classify the outcome without shifting the candidate to a more convenient swing.

Avoid saying that a sweep proves engineering or manipulation. A candle chart records prices, not the identity or intent of the orders involved. You can test a sequence of visible events without proving that larger narrative.

## The useful learning question

Collect clear and ambiguous examples, then ask whether IDM changes a decision compared with ordinary structure marking. Does it determine a specific wait condition, cancel an entry, or define a reference level? If it does none of these, leave it as descriptive vocabulary until a practical rule is available.
