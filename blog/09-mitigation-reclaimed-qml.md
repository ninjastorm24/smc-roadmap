# Mitigation, reclaimed levels and QML: describe the events before the label

Compare zone reuse with a reversal sequence while keeping source differences visible.

**Lesson 09 · Week 4 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/09-mitigation-reclaimed-qml.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-4)

A market returns to an old level. Some chart readers call the area mitigation, others describe a reclaimed block, and another may see a Quasimodo pattern. Those labels are not interchangeable. They can depend on different earlier events and select different boundaries.

Start with a chronological description: which level existed, how it was crossed, whether it was regained and whether a retest occurred. Then apply the source’s label.

## Mitigation needs a chosen definition

The workbook asks you to use one consistent mitigation-block source. The [TTrades explanation](https://ttrades.com/mitigation-blocks-simplified/) discusses a failure-based zone and distinguishes its conditions from a breaker interpretation. Other educators can organize these events differently.

Write the swing sequence, qualifying candle and required structural event directly into your concept note. If the method requires a failure swing rather than an earlier extreme being swept, record that distinction. Avoid filling the definition with a generic story that institutions are returning to settle positions; that intent is not observable from candles alone.

## Reclaimed describes a relationship, not a complete method

To reclaim a level is descriptively to regain it after losing it. Your research rule still needs to say what “lost” and “regained” mean.

Suppose a hypothetical level is 100. A wick below 100 followed by a close above it is one candidate event. Several closes below 100, followed by a later close above and retest, is another. Treating both as the same setup without recording the difference can conceal important execution changes.

The workbook does not supply the private instructor’s complete reclaimed-block method. Keep that exact model partially mapped until its formal conditions are available.

## QML has a structural sequence

For the workbook’s bearish Quasimodo example, begin with a high, then a higher high, a break of a relevant structural low and a possible return toward the earlier high or shoulder area. Mirror the relationships for a bullish case.

A hypothetical sequence could be high 110, intervening low 104, higher high 113 and then a qualifying close below 104. The earlier high near 110 becomes a candidate reference for a later return. The retest and the entry trigger remain separate requirements.

Without the low break, the bearish sequence is incomplete. Without a revisit, a retest entry is unavailable. If the chosen invalidation occurs first, preserve the failure rather than selecting a new shoulder afterward.

## One worksheet, three descriptions

For each example, record the raw price events first. Then identify which label qualifies under which source. Save cases in which only one label applies and cases in which none does.

Your strongest explanation should still make sense after the terminology is removed. That is a useful check that you understand the sequence rather than just recognizing an attractive shape.
