# Fibonacci, OTE and projections: the anchor rule comes first

Use ratios as explicit measurements while keeping SD and AO-X interpretation limits visible.

**Lesson 22 · Week 6 · About 3 minutes**

[Series index](README.md) · [Lesson worksheet](../lessons/22-fibonacci-sd-aox-ote.md) · [Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-6)

A Fibonacci tool can produce precise-looking numbers from a subjective starting choice. If the anchors move whenever an outcome disappoints, the ratios become difficult to evaluate.

The workbook’s Fibonacci lesson is most useful as a measurement exercise: define the swing, record when it became known and compare any proposed ratio filter against a simpler baseline.

## Separate measurement from prediction

A retracement ratio describes where price sits relative to two selected endpoints. It does not, by arithmetic alone, establish that a reversal will occur there.

For a hypothetical upward leg from 100 to 120, the leg length is 20. A 50% retracement is at 110. A 62% retracement is at 107.6, and a 79% retracement is at 104.2. Thus the commonly taught 62%–79% OTE band lies approximately between 104.2 and 107.6 for these anchors.

These numbers follow a specified convention: retracement is measured down from the leg’s high. Software orientation and custom settings should be checked rather than assumed.

## Premium/discount is a broader classification

Under the same 100–120 range, everything below 110 is discount relative to that range. The OTE interval is a narrower proposed band within it. Neither classification supplies an entry trigger by itself.

If you use a different dealing range for premium/discount, record it separately. Two tools drawn from different anchors may appear to disagree because they are measuring different moves.

## Treat SD and AO-X carefully

The workbook offers standard-deviation projections as one possible public match for SD, without confirming the instructor’s expansion. If a projection tool uses multiples of a selected leg, document that construction. Do not assume the label represents an estimated statistical distribution or a calibrated probability interval.

AO-X remains undefined in the available syllabus. Its anchor, levels and role cannot be reconstructed from its name. Leave the component unresolved rather than substituting an unrelated indicator.

## Compare the filter with its absence

[TTrades’ OTE explanation](https://ttrades.com/optimal-trade-entry-explained-how-to-use-ote-like-a-pro/) introduces the public retracement convention. Read [Adam Grimes’s critique](https://www.adamhgrimes.com/whats-wrong-fibonacci/) alongside it to examine assumptions about flexible measurement and ratio significance.

For a development exercise, freeze your baseline model and anchor-selection rule. Compare its eligible opportunities with the subset that also satisfies the OTE condition. Record opportunities excluded by the filter, not just the trades retained.

Report sample size, costs and outcomes under both rules. If you revise the filter after seeing results, version it and reserve new evaluation data. A precisely drawn band becomes useful evidence only when the decisions around it are equally precise.
