# Solo Learning Roadmap: Market Structure, Liquidity, and SMC/ICT Concepts

## 1. Purpose and how to use this roadmap

This roadmap turns the topics in the three supplied course screenshots into a structured self-study curriculum. The goal is to develop a repeatable process for reading charts, forming a hypothesis, testing an entry model, and reviewing results.

**Suggested pace: 24 weeks, 7–10 hours per week.** This is a study schedule, not a promise of trading readiness or profitability. Repeat a week whenever its checkpoint is incomplete. Use historical charts, replay, and simulated trading for the exercises.

The screenshots show 26 recorded technical classes, a broader technical/fundamental topic list, and a list of advanced entry techniques. They do not provide the actual lessons, entry rules, risk-management syllabus, psychology syllabus, or fundamental lessons. This roadmap therefore combines:

- **Source topics:** names visible in the screenshots.
- **Suggested study structure:** sequencing, exercises, checkpoints, journaling, and evaluation added for independent learning.
- **Items requiring clarification:** proprietary names and ambiguous acronyms that cannot be reconstructed reliably from titles alone.

SMC/ICT terminology is not universally standardized. Treat labels such as order blocks, liquidity sweeps, and inducement as chart-analysis hypotheses to define and test, rather than proof of institutional intent. A chart pattern alone does not establish a profitable edge.

## 2. Your study setup

Before starting, prepare:

1. A charting platform with historical candles and, preferably, replay.
2. One market and one instrument to study consistently at first.
3. Three fixed timeframes: one for context, one for setup identification, and one for execution practice. Choose these around the amount of time you can realistically observe charts.
4. A journal in a spreadsheet or notes application.
5. Folders for annotated screenshots: `structure`, `liquidity`, `zones`, `bias`, `entries`, and `reviews`.
6. A source log containing lesson titles, URLs or book references, dates studied, and unresolved questions.

Record the instrument's session hours, chart timezone, price increment, simulated transaction costs, and any session-specific behavior you intend to study. Be consistent about whether screenshots show bid, ask, or last-traded prices when that information is available.

### Required journal fields

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Date and instrument | Include timezone and session |
| Model version | The exact rule set used for this example |
| Context | Higher-timeframe structure and relevant range |
| Hypothesis | Expected behavior and what would contradict it |
| Liquidity references | Levels marked before revealing the outcome |
| Point of interest | Zone boundaries and why the zone qualifies |
| Trigger | Observable event required before entry |
| Invalidation | Price or condition that makes the setup wrong |
| Planned exit | Target and any time-based exit rule |
| Risk unit | Planned loss represented as 1R |
| Outcome | Result in R after modeled costs |
| Rule adherence | Followed, missed, or violated; explain why |
| Evidence | Before-and-after screenshots |
| Review | One lesson and one open question |

Keep observation-only examples separate from simulated trades. Do not convert a successful-looking chart into a trade record after seeing its outcome.

## 3. Weekly routine

| Activity | Time per week | Output |
|---|---:|---|
| Study a lesson or credible explanation | 2 hours | Short notes in your own words |
| Define rules and terminology | 1 hour | Updated glossary and checklist |
| Annotate historical charts | 2 hours | Labeled examples and counterexamples |
| Replay without future candles | 1–3 hours | Timestamped decisions |
| Review | 1–2 hours | Weekly scorecard and next steps |

For a long class, split the video across sessions. Pause to annotate charts instead of treating completion of a video as mastery.

At the end of every week, answer:

- Can I explain the concept without copying the lesson?
- Can I identify it before seeing the next candles?
- Can I state what does **not** qualify?
- Did I record failures and ambiguous examples?
- Which rule is still too subjective to test consistently?

The example counts below are practice targets, not statistical proof of an edge.

## 4. Phase 1 — Foundations and structure: weeks 1–4

### Week 1: Orientation and chart basics

**Course reference:** Class 01 — Orientation.

**Study**

- Candlestick open, high, low, and close; timeframe aggregation.
- Trending, ranging, and transitional price behavior.
- Basic order types, spread, slippage, fees, and simulated fills.
- The difference between a description of past price and a rule usable in real time.

**Practice**

1. Configure your instrument, timezone, and three timeframes.
2. Annotate 10 charts showing different market conditions.
3. Start a glossary with a definition, source, example, and counterexample for each term.
4. Write down your available study hours and the conditions under which you will skip a session.

**Deliverable:** One-page study plan and a working journal.

**Checkpoint:** You can describe a chart plainly without relying on SMC terminology.

### Week 2: Pullbacks, swings, and swing hierarchy

**Course references:** Class 02 — Pullback Identify; Class 03 — Swing Identify.

**Study**

- Swing highs and lows; higher highs, higher lows, lower highs, and lower lows.
- Pullback versus a change in the larger structure.
- Internal versus external swings.
- How much later information is needed to confirm a swing under your chosen definition.

**Practice**

1. Label swings on 20 chart segments.
2. Write an explicit swing-confirmation rule.
3. Repeat the exercise on the same segments after two days and compare your labels.
4. In replay, record the candle where each swing becomes knowable, not just the candle where it formed.

**Deliverable:** Swing-marking rules with at least five ambiguous examples.

**Checkpoint:** Your labels remain reasonably consistent across repeated reviews.

### Week 3: Breaks of structure and inducement

**Course reference:** Class 04 — Inducement (IDM).

**Source topics:** BOS, CHOCH, inducement, high-probability IDM.

**Study**

- Working definitions for break of structure and change of character.
- Wick penetration versus candle-close confirmation.
- How the course distinguishes inducement from an ordinary swing.
- The evidence needed to classify a break as continuation, reversal, or unresolved.

**Practice**

1. Collect 15 structure-break examples and 10 failures or ambiguous breaks.
2. Mark the relevant levels before revealing the next candles.
3. Record alternative interpretations where more than one swing matters.
4. Do not use “high probability” as a journal classification unless a defined filter has supporting test results.

**Deliverable:** A decision checklist for structure breaks and candidate inducement levels.

**Checkpoint:** You can identify the exact observation that would invalidate each label.

### Week 4: Advanced structure and fractal behavior

**Course references:** Class 05 — Advanced Market Structure; Class 10 — STL/STH/ITL/ITH Mapping; Class 14 — Fractal Nature.

**Study**

- Nested structure across your three timeframes.
- Short-term and intermediate-term high/low labels, using the instructor's definitions.
- Conflicting directional signals across timeframes.
- Why an apparent reversal on one timeframe can remain a pullback on another.

**Practice**

1. Build 10 top-down chart studies.
2. Annotate both continuation and reversal possibilities.
3. Describe the evidence needed to move from one scenario to the other.

**Deliverable:** A reusable top-down structure template.

**Phase checkpoint:** Explain structure, swing confirmation, and invalidation on an unseen historical chart without revealing future candles.

## 5. Phase 2 — Zones and points of interest: weeks 5–8

### Week 5: FVG, iFVG, and imbalance

**Course reference:** Class 06 — FVG, iFVG, Order Block/Imbalance.

**Study:** The source's definitions for fair value gaps, inverse fair value gaps, imbalance, displacement, zone boundaries, and invalidation. Do not assume different sources use identical definitions.

**Practice**

- Annotate 20 candidate zones, including ones that fail or are never revisited.
- Record the formation time, first revisit, reaction, and invalidation.
- Distinguish a visual gap pattern from a complete entry setup.
- Use a fixed observation window so a zone is not called successful merely because price eventually reacts.

**Deliverable:** FVG/iFVG checklist and example library.

**Checkpoint:** Two reviews of the same chart produce similar boundaries and classifications.

### Week 6: Order blocks and advanced order blocks

**Course references:** Classes 06–07.

**Study:** Candidate order-block selection, required structural context, displacement filters, first revisit versus repeated revisits, and invalidation.

**Practice**

- Collect 20 candidates using one fixed definition.
- Write down why nearby candles do or do not qualify.
- Compare qualifying zones that react with zones that fail.
- Record whether each extra filter makes selection clearer or simply explains past winners.

**Deliverable:** One order-block rule sheet.

**Checkpoint:** You can select a candidate before seeing the reaction.

### Week 7: Rejection, breaker, mitigation, and reclaimed blocks

**Course references:** Class 08 — Rejection Block, Breaker Block, Unicorn Module; Class 09 — Mitigation & Reclaimed Block, QML.

**Study:** Each named pattern's formation sequence, structural prerequisite, invalidation, and relationship to previously studied zones. Verify the course's meaning of Unicorn and QML.

**Practice**

- Create a comparison table: pattern, formation rule, confirmation, invalidation, common confusion.
- Find at least five examples of each pattern you can define reliably.
- Put unclear labels in an unresolved list rather than inventing a rule.

**Deliverable:** A block-pattern comparison sheet with screenshots.

**Checkpoint:** You can explain why a chart belongs to one category rather than assigning every possible label to it.

### Week 8: Structure-based POI and supply/demand

**Course references:** Class 11 — Structure Based POI; relevant topics from Class 13.

**Study:** Point-of-interest selection, zone overlap, premium/discount within a defined range, and source-specific supply/demand rules.

**Practice**

1. Mark no more than two candidate POIs per chart on 15 charts.
2. Record the range anchors used for premium/discount.
3. Rank each POI using a short checklist established in advance.
4. Include “no suitable POI” as a valid result.

**Deliverable:** POI selection checklist.

**Phase checkpoint:** Complete a top-down chart plan with structure, a justified POI, and clear invalidation.

## 6. Phase 3 — Liquidity, ranges, and bias: weeks 9–12

### Week 9: Liquidity vocabulary and mapping

**Course references:** Class 12 — Liquidity: External, Internal, Grab & Sweep; Class 13 — Liquidity Pool, Void, Supply & Demand, Premium & Discount.

**Study:** Buy-side and sell-side liquidity labels, internal/external range liquidity, equal highs/lows, liquidity pools, voids, and the course's distinction between a grab and a sweep.

**Practice**

- Map potential liquidity references on 20 charts before replay.
- Track whether price reaches, passes through, rejects, or never visits each reference during your fixed window.
- Separate observable price behavior from assumptions about actual resting orders.

**Deliverable:** Liquidity annotation guide and 20 chart studies.

**Checkpoint:** Your map distinguishes observed levels from a prediction about their future use.

### Week 10: Dealing ranges and premium/discount

**Course references:** Classes 13 and 16; internal range play from the broader topic list.

**Study:** Range-anchor selection, midpoint, internal/external references, range expansion, and conflicting ranges across timeframes.

**Practice**

- Annotate 15 ranges with a written anchor-selection rule.
- Record when each anchor becomes confirmed.
- Compare continuation, range-bound, and failed-range outcomes.
- Update a range only when your predefined conditions allow it.

**Deliverable:** Range-selection rules and a range-update log.

**Checkpoint:** You do not redraw a range solely to make a past move fit the narrative.

### Week 11: Daily bias

**Course reference:** Class 15 — Advanced BIAS Part 01 / Daily Bias.

**Study:** Higher-timeframe context, candidate destinations, relevant POIs, contradictory evidence, and neutral conditions.

**Practice**

For 10 historical sessions, stop replay before the session and record:

1. Bullish, bearish, or neutral working bias.
2. Evidence supporting the hypothesis.
3. Evidence against it.
4. The condition that cancels or changes it.
5. What would make the session unsuitable for your model.

**Deliverable:** Ten pre-session plans with post-session reviews.

**Checkpoint:** Judge the quality of the reasoning separately from whether the direction happened to be right.

### Week 12: Time, price, and applied bias

**Course references:** Class 16 — Time and Price Theory / Dealing Range; Class 17 — How to Apply Bias on Chart (Advanced).

**Study:** Session context, time windows, daily range development, and how bias connects to a specific setup.

**Practice**

- Run 10 replay sessions using a fixed observation window.
- Log timezone and any daylight-saving changes affecting the market session.
- Require structure, POI, and trigger agreement before a simulated entry.
- Record neutral or no-entry sessions as completed work.

**Deliverable:** A one-page pre-session planning template.

**Phase checkpoint:** Produce a plan before the outcome and follow its invalidation rules without rewriting the original notes.

## 7. Phase 4 — Entry models and execution rules: weeks 13–16

### Week 13: Strategy architecture and structure-shift models

**Course references:** Class 18 — My Personal Trading Strategy; Class 19 — MSS, CISD, TBS and TWS.

The instructor's personal strategy is not available from the screenshots. Treat it as a source to study only if you have legitimate access; otherwise build a clearly labeled independent model.

**Study:** Source definitions for MSS, CISD, TBS, and TWS; their relationship to context, trigger, and invalidation. Do not assume the acronyms alone specify an entry.

**Practice**

- Separate each model into context → location → trigger → invalidation → exit.
- Choose one model with clear, observable rules.
- Collect 15 qualifying and 10 nonqualifying examples.

**Deliverable:** First draft of one complete entry model.

### Week 14: AMD, Judas swing, and CRT + TBS

**Course references:** Class 20 — AMD and Judas Swing; Class 21 — CRT + TBS Entry Technic.

**Study:** The source's sequence rules, session assumptions, confirmation requirements, and failure conditions.

**Practice**

- Collect 10 examples of each model you can define.
- Record sequences that begin plausibly but fail to complete.
- Compare the models with your week-13 model without merging all their filters.

**Deliverable:** Model comparison table and a provisional primary model.

### Week 15: Fibonacci tools and quarterly theory

**Course references:** Class 22 — Fib Tool: SD, AO-X, P&D, OTE; Class 23 — Quarterly Theory.

**Study:** Source-specific meanings of SD and AO-X, Fibonacci anchor rules, premium/discount, OTE, and the exact time segmentation used in quarterly theory. These titles do not provide enough information to recreate the instructor's rules.

**Practice**

- State anchor rules before measuring any move.
- Compare your primary model with and without one proposed filter at a time.
- Log the timezone and interval boundaries for any time-based framework.
- Retain the simpler model unless the added rule has a clear, testable purpose.

**Deliverable:** Optional-filter notes, including unresolved terminology.

## 8. Phase 5 — Risk, fundamentals, and psychology: weeks 17–20

The screenshots show Risk Management and Psychology tabs but not their contents. The following is a suggested independent curriculum, not a transcription of those tabs. Start basic simulated risk limits in week 1; use this phase to formalize them.

### Week 17: Risk units and simulated position sizing

**Study**

- Planned loss as 1R; realized losses may exceed it when fills differ.
- Stop distance, unit value, fees, spread, and slippage.
- Total open risk and exposure to closely related instruments.
- Limits for a session and rules for stopping after execution errors.

For a simple linear instrument, a starting calculation is:

`simulated quantity = simulated risk budget / loss per unit at the planned stop`

Instrument-specific contract multipliers, currency conversion, size increments, and costs must be handled separately. Do not apply this simplified formula unchanged to nonlinear products.

**Practice:** Work through 20 simulated sizing examples and verify the arithmetic using the platform's contract specifications. Document how costs and rounding affect the planned loss.

**Deliverable:** A written simulation risk policy with per-trade, concurrent-exposure, and session limits selected before testing.

### Week 18: Fundamentals and event context

**Source topics:** Fundamental Analysis, Fundamental, and Live Market Analysis. No detailed fundamental syllabus is visible.

**Study**

- The events relevant to your chosen instrument.
- Scheduled announcements versus unexpected news.
- How wider spreads, rapid price movement, and gaps affect execution assumptions.
- The difference between explaining an event afterward and predicting its market impact beforehand.

**Practice**

- Use official release calendars and primary announcements for your selected market.
- Annotate 10 historical event windows.
- Decide in advance whether your simulation excludes certain event windows or studies them separately.
- Avoid interpreting historical event reactions as guaranteed future behavior.

**Deliverable:** Event-context checklist and documented event-window policy.

### Week 19: Psychology and process discipline

**Study:** Hindsight bias, fear of missing out, revenge trading, overconfidence after wins, and changing rules after a small losing sample.

**Practice**

- Log emotional state before and after each simulated session.
- Track rule violations separately from losing trades.
- Write a reset routine after a missed setup, loss, or execution error.
- Review five well-executed losses and five poorly executed wins if your sample contains them; do not manufacture examples to fill a quota.

**Deliverable:** Personal execution checklist and reset routine.

### Week 20: Freeze the playbook

Write a complete primary-model specification containing:

1. Instrument, session, timezone, and timeframes.
2. Context and bias rules.
3. POI and liquidity definitions.
4. Exact trigger and order assumptions.
5. Invalidation, planned exit, and time-based exit rules.
6. Position-sizing and exposure rules for simulation.
7. Event exclusions and other no-entry conditions.
8. Cost assumptions and ambiguous-fill treatment.
9. Screenshot and journal requirements.
10. Model version and date frozen.

**Deliverable:** Playbook version 1.0.

**Phase checkpoint:** Every simulated trade can be classified as eligible or ineligible using the playbook before its outcome is known.

## 9. Phase 6 — Testing and forward simulation: weeks 21–24+

### Week 21: Historical development sample

- Select a continuous historical period before inspecting individual setups.
- Record every qualifying opportunity, including losses and skipped entries.
- Aim for 30–50 examples as an initial workflow exercise; extend the calendar if your model is infrequent.
- Include realistic costs and conservative assumptions for unclear intrabar order fills.
- If rules need refinement, revise them openly and version the changes.

**Deliverable:** Development dataset and a record of rule revisions.

### Week 22: Separate evaluation sample

- Freeze the revised rules before opening a different historical period.
- Record all qualifying opportunities under the same rules.
- Include different market conditions where possible.
- Do not optimize rules on this sample and continue calling it untouched evaluation data. Any revision requires a new evaluation period.

**Deliverable:** Separate evaluation report with limitations.

### Weeks 23–24: Forward simulation

- Observe new sessions without future information.
- Use the same playbook and logging process.
- Track missed entries, latency, spread changes, and differences from historical testing.
- Extend beyond week 24 when needed to collect a useful sample; two weeks can be too short for an infrequent setup.

**Deliverable:** Forward-simulation journal and a written continue/refine/archive decision.

### Metrics to review

| Metric | Definition or use |
|---|---|
| Sample size | Number of qualifying opportunities and executed simulations |
| Win/loss/breakeven counts | Use consistent classifications after costs |
| Mean net R | Sum of net trade outcomes in R divided by trade count |
| Average win and loss | Magnitude of winning and losing outcomes |
| Profit factor | Gross positive outcomes divided by absolute gross negative outcomes; undefined with no losses |
| Maximum drawdown | Largest peak-to-trough decline in the simulated result series; state whether measured in R or currency |
| Longest losing streak | Useful for understanding the observed sample, not a maximum future bound |
| Rule adherence | Percentage of simulated trades that followed all required rules |
| Opportunity capture | Eligible setups executed versus all eligible setups observed |
| Cost sensitivity | Whether conclusions change under less favorable cost assumptions |
| Condition breakdown | Results by session or market condition, with subgroup sample counts |

If computing expectancy from win/loss rates, include breakeven outcomes consistently and do not subtract costs twice. Mean net R directly captures the average of your recorded net outcomes.

**Completion checkpoint:** You have a reproducible model, a clean journal, separate development and evaluation records, and forward-simulation evidence. Positive results in a small sample do not establish a durable edge. Completing this roadmap is an educational milestone, not an instruction to trade live.

## 11. How to study without access to the course

1. Start with market mechanics, order types, and contract specifications from the relevant exchange, broker documentation, or charting platform documentation.
2. Use one consistent educational source for each SMC concept and record its exact definition.
3. Compare conflicting definitions in your glossary rather than mixing them inside one test.
4. Prefer resources showing complete rules, invalidation, and failed examples over highlight reels.
5. Skip proprietary modules that cannot be independently defined. The core roadmap remains usable with structure, liquidity references, POI selection, and one testable entry model.
6. Keep any independently developed rules clearly labeled as your own; they are not a reconstruction of the instructor's private strategy.

## 13. Completion checklist

- [ ] I can mark swings and structure consistently.
- [ ] I distinguish confirmed information from hindsight.
- [ ] I can define a POI and its invalidation before a revisit.
- [ ] I can map liquidity references without claiming certainty about hidden orders.
- [ ] I can write a bias hypothesis and acknowledge conflicting evidence.
- [ ] I have one complete entry model with objective eligibility rules.
- [ ] I understand simulated sizing, costs, and exposure limits.
- [ ] I document event context and no-entry conditions.
- [ ] I record failed setups, skipped opportunities, and process mistakes.
- [ ] I keep development and evaluation samples separate.
- [ ] I have completed forward simulation and documented its limitations.
- [ ] I can explain why the model should be studied further, revised, or archived.

The intended end product is a documented research process: a glossary, annotated chart library, one playbook, and an honest evaluation record.
