# Lesson 02 — Pullback Identification

**Study budget:** 45–75 minutes learning + 45–60 minutes chart lab, plus review. Extend the week when the evidence takes longer.

## Outcome

Distinguish an ordinary retracement from a deeper structural reversal.

## Study in order

1. Define impulse, expansion, pullback, consolidation and reversal in your own words. Mark the dominant directional leg before labeling any correction.
2. On a bullish chart, identify the last impulsive move and its retracement. Note which prior swing is protected by the current structure.
3. Compare shallow, deep and overlapping pullbacks. A larger retracement is not automatically a trend reversal without structural evidence.
4. Observe whether the pullback reaches an FVG, prior support/resistance or another previously identified area, without assuming it must reverse there.
5. Create a rule for when a pullback has ended; compare a close above a minor high, a displacement candle and a higher-low confirmation.
6. Repeat on a downtrend and a sideways market. Identify three cases where an apparent pullback became a reversal.

## Chart lab

- [ ] Annotate 15 bullish and 15 bearish impulse/pullback sequences.
- [ ] For five examples, freeze the chart before the next candle and predict only whether your rule is triggered.
- [ ] Label the invalidation swing for each sequence.

## Mastery check

Given a chart, explain which level would turn your pullback interpretation into a reversal hypothesis.

**Watch for:** Do not call every red candle in an uptrend a valid pullback.
