# Lesson 12 — Liquidity: External, Internal, Grab and Sweep

[Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-3) · [All lessons](README.md) · [Progress](../PROGRESS.md)

**Week 3 · Original class runtime: 1h 40m 40s**

**Source:** Adapted directly from the DOCX class sheet. Its six study steps, chart lab and mastery check are preserved below. Public links teach related concepts; they do not reproduce a private class.

**Study budget:** 45–75 minutes learning + 45–60 minutes chart lab, plus review. Extend the week when the evidence takes longer.

## Outcome

Map visible liquidity references and distinguish sweep, breakout and larger external liquidity events.

## Study in order

1. Define buy-side liquidity (above highs) and sell-side liquidity (below lows) as hypotheses about where stops may cluster.
2. Identify external-range levels (important prior highs/lows) and internal-range levels (minor pivots and imbalances inside the range).
3. Mark previous day high/low, session high/low and equal high/low levels before moving to lower timeframes.
4. Distinguish a wick through a level with close back inside (sweep candidate) from price accepting beyond it (possible breakout).
5. Define "grab" versus "sweep" for your own journal; the industry does not have one universal boundary.
6. Track what price does after the event; avoid automatically interpreting every break as manipulation.

## Chart lab

- [ ] Mark BSL and SSL on 20 charts before the later candles are revealed.
- [ ] Collect 10 sweeps and 10 sustained breakouts using one close-back rule.
- [ ] Write the next visible target, or "no clear target", on each sample.

## Mastery check

How do you tell a sweep hypothesis from a confirmed structural breakout in real time?

**Watch for:** A price chart does not show the actual placement or ownership of unseen stop orders.
