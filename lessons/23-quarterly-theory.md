**Source:** Adapted directly from the DOCX class sheet. Its six study steps, chart lab and mastery check are preserved below. Public links teach related concepts; they do not reproduce a private class.

**Study budget:** 45–75 minutes learning + 45–60 minutes chart lab, plus review. Extend the week when the evidence takes longer.

## Outcome

Learn four-part time segmentation and test it as a context filter, not a forecasting law.

## Study in order

1. Read quarterly theory (often associated with Daye): divide a selected year/month/week/day/session cycle into four quarters.
2. Understand the AMDX labels - accumulation, manipulation, distribution, continuation/reversal - as a proposed classification.
3. Write the calendar and session anchor conventions before charting; different resources disagree on some "true open" details.
4. Mark New York midnight, relevant quarter boundaries and the day's liquidity references on five sample days.
5. Compare quarter timing with actual volatility/expansion without relabeling phases to fit outcome.
6. Track nonconforming days and daylight-saving changes; treat timing claims as hypotheses requiring market-specific tests.

## Chart lab

- [ ] Prepare a quarter timeline for 10 days on your chosen instrument.
- [ ] Before replaying each session, annotate the quarter and expected scenario.
- [ ] Count days that follow, partly follow or contradict your written phase rules.

## Mastery check

Can the model be falsified on a day when expansion happens in a different quarter?

**Watch for:** Quarterly theory has no broadly established independent evidence of a universal four-phase edge.
