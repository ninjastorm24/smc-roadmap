**Source:** Adapted directly from the DOCX class sheet. Its six study steps, chart lab and mastery check are preserved below. Public links teach related concepts; they do not reproduce a private class.

**Study budget:** 45–75 minutes learning + 45–60 minutes chart lab, plus review. Extend the week when the evidence takes longer.

## Outcome

Add time-of-day and range context without confusing timing windows with trade signals.

## Study in order

1. Define a relevant dealing range and its equilibrium; explicitly state which two highs/lows you used.
2. Mark previous-day and session OHLC levels, Asian range, London/NY references and scheduled market openings.
3. Read about kill zones and observe volatility differences by hour in your instrument. Convert times using New York timezone and daylight saving.
4. Study premium/discount and expected draw on liquidity as separate conditions: both can disagree.
5. Record news and holiday effects and whether a time window contained a clear setup, a fake move or no signal.
6. Create a time-price checklist with range, bias, session, liquidity reference, confirmation and invalidation.

## Chart lab

- [ ] Prepare time-and-price maps for 10 historical days.
- [ ] Document at least five no-trade windows despite having a directional bias.
- [ ] Label equivalent Bangladesh clock times for your chosen session dates (DST matters).

## Mastery check

Why is "London open" not always the same fixed Dhaka clock time?

**Watch for:** Quarter hours, kill zones and opening prices should be treated as contextual hypotheses, not deterministic schedules.
