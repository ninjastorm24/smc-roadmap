# Lesson 13 — Liquidity Pool, Void, Supply/Demand and Premium/Discount

[Roadmap](../SOLO_LEARNING_ROADMAP.md#week-3) · [All lessons](README.md) · [Progress](../PROGRESS.md)

**Week 3 · Original class runtime: 1h 31m 19s**

**Source:** Adapted directly from the DOCX class sheet. Its six study steps, chart lab and mastery check are preserved below. Public links teach related concepts; they do not reproduce a private class.

**Study budget:** 45–75 minutes learning + 45–60 minutes chart lab, plus review. Extend the week when the evidence takes longer.

## Outcome

Combine clusters of reference levels, low-activity zones and position within a dealing range.

## Study in order

1. Mark equal highs/lows and repeated swing extremes as visible liquidity pools, not guaranteed stop concentrations.
2. Differentiate an FVG from a broader liquidity void or rapid price run; write the boundary method you choose.
3. Study basic supply/demand zones as areas preceding directional moves, then compare those zones with OB definitions.
4. Define a dealing range using two chosen structural extremes. Split the range at its 50% equilibrium (EQ).
5. Label premium above EQ and discount below EQ. Explain that these are relative prices, not automatic buy/sell signals.
6. Combine a liquidity draw, zone and premium/discount context into a directional scenario with an explicit invalidation.

## Mastery check

Why do premium/discount labels change when the reference range changes?

**Watch for:** Do not claim a zone is "institutional" without evidence beyond price behavior.
